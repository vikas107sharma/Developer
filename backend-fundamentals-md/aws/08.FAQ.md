Q: Let's say I have 2 containers and on load I spin up 2 more. Will ECS have sufficient resources to spin up 2 more? If yes, then why not keep 4 containers running from the beginning, since it already has the resources for 4 containers?

To answer this, we need to look at how AWS ECS operates depending on your launch type: EC2 or Fargate.

--------------------------------------------------
Scenario 1: ECS on EC2
--------------------------------------------------
Suppose you have one EC2 instance with 8 GB RAM.

Resource Allocation:
- Total RAM: 8 GB
- RAM per container/task: 2 GB
- Tasks running initially: 2
- RAM currently used by tasks: 4 GB
- RAM remaining (before OS and other overhead): 4 GB

In this scenario, ECS can potentially start two more tasks because the underlying EC2 instance has enough available memory. 

But why not run four from the beginning?
Because you may not need them yet. Running two tasks initially allows you to utilize the remaining capacity on that EC2 instance for other applications or services. If you dedicated all resources to 4 containers from the start, those resources would be locked up and wasted if traffic is low.

--------------------------------------------------
Scenario 2: ECS on Fargate
--------------------------------------------------
Fargate works differently because it is serverless. You don't manage or pay for underlying EC2 instances directly.

When ECS scales from 2 tasks to 4 on Fargate, AWS automatically provisions the compute resources for the additional tasks on the fly (subject to quotas and available capacity). You only pay for the exact resources used by those specific tasks while they are running.

You don't need to keep spare capacity yourself, though you should note that scaling up does take a little bit of time for the tasks to spin up.

--------------------------------------------------
The Core Reason: Cost Optimization
--------------------------------------------------
Then why not keep 4 tasks running from the beginning in either scenario?
Because unused capacity costs money.

Suppose your traffic varies throughout the day:

| Time       | Required Tasks | Reason         |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| Night      | 2              | Low traffic    |
| Afternoon  | 4              | Higher traffic |
| Sale Event | 10             | Traffic spike  |

With auto scaling, ECS dynamically adjusts the task count according to your configured policy. You can set a minimum of 2 and a maximum of 10 tasks. This keeps your costs low during quiet periods while ensuring you have additional capacity exactly when demand rises.

--------------------------------------------------
One Final Distinction to Remember
--------------------------------------------------
- ECS Service Auto Scaling: Changes the number of application tasks (containers) running.
- EC2 Capacity Auto Scaling: Adds or removes actual EC2 instances when the cluster itself needs more or less overall compute capacity.
