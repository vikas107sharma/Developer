4. Hold on -> Wait for a short time.

One moment, please. (most formal)
Just a moment.

6. Let me do it by myself -> Let me do it without anyone's help.

7. By the way -> "By the way" = "Oh, one more thing...". Used to introduce a new or related topic without changing the conversation abruptly.

By the way, I sent you the email yesterday.


8. Can you spare a minute 

Can you spare a minute to review my code?

9. Either -> Also not / one or the other (depends on the context).

Too → Positive sentences.
I like coffee. I like tea too.
Either → Negative sentences.
I don't like coffee. I don't like tea either.


10. Exactly = “That is correct / precisely”
Use it when agreeing with or confirming someone's statement.

Example:

A: So the issue is because the webhook isn't reaching our server?
B: Exactly.

A: You mean the payment is already verified?
B: Exactly.


2. Certainly = “Yes, definitely / of course”

Use it when agreeing to do something or confirming that something can be done. It is more formal than “sure.”

Example:

A: Can you check this?
B: Certainly.

A: Can you deploy this today?
B: Certainly, I'll take care of it.

Indeed = “Yes, that is true”

Use it when strongly agreeing with or confirming a fact. It sounds more formal and is less common in casual workplace chat.

Example:

A: This is a production issue.
B: Indeed.

A: The implementation is more complex than expected.
B: Indeed, it is.

| Word           | Meaning                          | Use it when                                      | Example                                                    |
| -------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Exactly**    | “That’s precisely correct.”      | Someone has correctly understood something       | “So the issue is with the webhook?” → **Exactly.**         |
| **Certainly**  | “Yes, definitely / of course.”   | Someone asks you to do something                 | “Can you check this?” → **Certainly.**                     |
| **Indeed**     | “Yes, that is true.”             | Confirming a fact; sounds more formal            | “This is a complex flow.” → **Indeed.**                    |
| **Agreed**     | “I agree with this.”             | Agreeing with a suggestion, opinion, or decision | “We should discuss this with the client.” → **Agreed.**    |
| **Absolutely** | “Definitely / completely agree.” | Strongly agreeing or confirming                  | “This needs to be fixed before release.” → **Absolutely.** |


Exactly. → I completely agree. ✅
Okay. → I understand / I accept the plan. ✅
Sure. → I'm willing to do it. ⚠️ (Less natural here unless you're responding to a request.)
Certainly. → Yes, I'll do it. ⚠️ (Used mainly when someone asks you to do something.)


Quick tip:

Exactly = Agreement.
Okay = Acceptance.
Sure / Certainly = Response to a request.


11. Since when did you...? 
Asking from what time someone started doing something. It can also express surprise.

A: I love horror movies.
B: Since when did you like horror movies?


12. Although: In spite of the fact that
Although it was raining, we went out.


13. Even though: Despite the fact that (strongest contrast).
Even though it was raining heavily, we went out.


14. Yet: Until now (usually in negative sentences and questions).
I haven't heard this song yet.
The food is not arrived yet.


15. Still: Something continues to be true.
Are you still awake?
Its 10PM already, the food is still not here.


16. Would have: Something that would have happened, but didn't.
I would have helped you if I had known.


17. Could have: Something was possible, but didn't happen.
She could have become a doctor.


18. Should have: Something was the right thing to do, but didn't happen (often regret or criticism).
You should have told me earlier.


19. Either: One of two / also (in negative sentences).
I don't like coffee. I don't like tea either.


20. Neither: Not one and not the other.
A: I don't smoke.
B: Neither do I.


21. Rather: Preference / to some extent.
I'd rather stay home.
