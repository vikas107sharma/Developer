━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 💬 Useful Everyday Phrases ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

🔹 Here you go — this is what you wanted
A: Can I have your pen?
B: Here you go.

🔹 That's not necessary — no need to do that
A: Should I explain everything in detail?
B: That's not necessary.

🔹 Hold on — wait for a short time
One moment, please.   (most formal)
Just a moment.
Please wait a moment.
Give me a moment.      (less formal)

🔹 I decided — when you've already made a choice
Since UPI and NEFT have different behaviour, I decided to make them the same.

🔹 Let me do it by myself — without anyone's help
A: Should I fix your laptop?
B: No, thanks. Let me do it by myself.

🔹 By the way — "Oh, one more thing…"
Introduces a new/related topic without changing the conversation abruptly.
By the way, I sent you the email yesterday.

🔹 Can you spare a minute — do you have a little time?
Can you spare a minute to review my code?

🔹 Since when did you…? — from what time did you start? (can show surprise)
A: I love horror movies.
B: Since when did you like horror movies?


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ ✅ Agreement & Confirmation Words ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

🔹 Exactly — "That's precisely correct"
Use when agreeing with or confirming someone's statement.
A: So the issue is because the webhook isn't reaching our server?
B: Exactly.

🔹 Certainly — "Yes, definitely / of course" (more formal than "sure")
Use when agreeing to do something.
A: Can you deploy this today?
B: Certainly, I'll take care of it.

🔹 Indeed — "Yes, that is true" (formal, confirming a fact)
A: The implementation is more complex than expected.
B: Indeed, it is.

🔹 Quick reference
  Word        | Meaning                     | Use it when
  ----------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------
  Exactly     | That's precisely correct    | Someone understood correctly
  Certainly   | Yes, definitely / of course | Someone asks you to do something
  Indeed      | Yes, that is true           | Confirming a fact (formal)
  Agreed      | I agree with this           | Agreeing with a suggestion
  Absolutely  | Definitely / completely     | Strongly agreeing or confirming

🔹 Agreement vs acceptance vs response
Exactly.   -> I completely agree.                ✅
Okay.      -> I understand / I accept the plan.  ✅
Sure.      -> I'm willing to do it.              ⚠️ (response to a request)
Certainly. -> Yes, I'll do it.                   ⚠️ (when someone asks you)

💡 Tip:  Exactly = agreement · Okay = acceptance · Sure/Certainly = response to a request.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 🔀 Contrast Connectors ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

🔹 Although — in spite of the fact that
Although it was raining, we went out.

🔹 Even though — despite the fact that (strongest contrast)
Even though it was raining heavily, we went out.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ ⏳ Yet vs Still ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

🔹 Yet — until now (usually negatives & questions)
I haven't heard this song yet.
The food hasn't arrived yet.

🔹 Still — something continues to be true
Are you still awake?
It's 10 PM already, and the food is still not here.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 🕰️ Modal Perfect (would/could/should have) ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

🔹 Would have — would have happened, but didn't
I would have helped you if I had known.

🔹 Could have — was possible, but didn't happen
She could have become a doctor.

🔹 Should have — was the right thing to do, but didn't happen (regret / criticism)
You should have told me earlier.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ ➕ Either / Neither / Too / Rather ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

🔹 Too — in positive sentences
I like coffee. I like tea too.

🔹 Either — in negative sentences ("also not")
I don't like coffee. I don't like tea either.

🔹 Neither — not one and not the other
A: I don't smoke.
B: Neither do I.

🔹 Rather — preference / to some extent
I'd rather stay home.
